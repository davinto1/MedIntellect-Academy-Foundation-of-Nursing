Fundamentals of Nursing is the cornerstone of nursing education. It is both an art and a science.

“Putting the patient in the best condition for nature to act upon him.” — Florence Nightingale (1860)
“Assist the individual… in activities contributing to health or recovery that he would perform unaided if he had the necessary strength, will or knowledge.” — Virginia Henderson (1966)

ICN: Autonomous and collaborative care of individuals of all ages, families, groups and communities — health promotion, disease prevention, care of the ill/disabled/dying, advocacy, research, education, policy and management.

Goals of Nursing

Promotion of health • Prevention of disease • Restoration of health • Rehabilitation • Relief of pain & suffering • Comfort • Health education • Emotional support • Assistance with ADLs • Support for dignified death.

Nine Professional Roles

Caregiver • Advocate • Educator • Communicator • Counsellor • Leader • Researcher • Manager • Collaborator

Metaparadigm (4 Concepts)

  • Person – recipient of care (individual, family, group, community)
  • Environment – internal & external factors influencing health
  • Health – dynamic state of well-being (not merely absence of disease)
  • Nursing – actions, knowledge & relationships that help people achieve/maintain/recover health
  1. Physiological – oxygen, food, water, elimination, temperature, rest, activity
  2. Safety & Security – protection from harm, stability, freedom from fear
  3. Love & Belonging – affection, relationships, social connection
  4. Esteem – self-respect, recognition, sense of worth
  5. Self-Actualization – achieving potential, creativity, personal growth
Lower needs must be reasonably satisfied before higher needs become motivators. Nurses prioritise care accordingly.

Five Steps

Assessment → Diagnosis → Planning → Implementation → Evaluation

Key Ethical Principles

  • Autonomy – respect patient’s right to decide
  • Beneficence – do good
  • Non-maleficence – do no harm
  • Justice – fairness
  • Veracity – truthfulness
  • Confidentiality – protect private information
  • Accountability & Integrity

Domains of Learning

Cognitive (knowledge) • Psychomotor (skills) • Affective (attitudes/values)

SMART objectives: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound

Therapeutic Techniques

Active listening • Open-ended questions • Clarification • Reflection • Restating • Summarizing • Focusing • Silence • Offering self

Avoid (Non-therapeutic)

False reassurance • Unsolicited advice • Accusatory “why” questions • Changing subject • Judgments

  • Temperature (oral): ≈ 36.2–37.2°C (some sources 36.5–37.5°C)
  • Pulse: 60–100 bpm (Tachycardia >100 • Bradycardia <60)
  • Respiration: 12–20 /min
  • Blood Pressure: ≈ 120/80 mmHg
  • SpO₂: 95–100% (healthy adults)
  • Pain: PQRST – Provoking, Quality, Region/Radiation, Severity, Timing
Always explain procedure, wash hands, ensure privacy, use correct technique, compare with previous values, report abnormalities, document promptly.

Chain of Infection (6 links)

Infectious agent → Reservoir → Portal of exit → Mode of transmission → Portal of entry → Susceptible host

Medical vs Surgical Asepsis

Medical (Clean) – reduce number & spread of microbes
Surgical (Sterile) – eliminate all microbes including spores

Transmission-Based Precautions

  • Contact (MRSA, C. diff) – gloves + gown
  • Droplet (Influenza, Meningococcus) – surgical mask
  • Airborne (TB, Measles, Chickenpox) – N95 + isolation room

WHO Five Moments

Before patient • Before clean/aseptic • After body fluid • After patient • After surroundings

Sterilization Methods

Autoclave (moist heat 121°C 15–20 min) • Dry heat • Chemical • Ethylene oxide gas • Gamma radiation

Core Safety Rules

  • Perform medication checks (when removing, preparing, administering)
  • Verify patient identity
  • Never administer unlabelled medication
  • Document and monitor response
  • Respect and document refusal

IM Injection Sites

1. Upper outer quadrant of buttock (avoid sciatic nerve)
2. Outer aspect of thigh (vastus lateralis)
3. Deltoid

Dangerous Drugs

Locked cupboard • Controlled key custody • Accurate register • Checked at every shift change

Always read the label carefully. If in doubt, do not give. Check against the prescription.
  • Room air ≈ 21% oxygen
  • Nasal cannula: 1–6 L/min → FiO₂ ≈ 24–44%
  • Simple face mask: 5–10 L/min → FiO₂ ≈ 35–50% (min 5–6 L to prevent CO₂ rebreathing)
  • Partial rebreather: 8–15 L/min → FiO₂ ≈ 50–70%
  • Non-rebreather: 10–15+ L/min → FiO₂ ≈ 60–90%+
  • Venturi mask: Precise controlled FiO₂ (ideal for COPD)

Critical Safety

  • Oxygen supports combustion — no flames, smoking or petroleum products
  • Humidify at flows ≥ 4 L/min
  • Prolonged high FiO₂ (>50% for >24–48 h) risks oxygen toxicity
  • COPD target SpO₂: 88–92%

Practice Question Bank

Theory, Short-answer and MCQs. Answers are hidden until you reveal them or select an option.

T1
Define nursing.
Answer
Nursing is a dynamic profession dedicated to promoting health, preventing illness, restoring health and alleviating suffering while addressing the physical, psychological, social, cultural and spiritual needs of patients.
T2
State Florence Nightingale’s definition of nursing.
Answer
Putting the patient in the best condition for nature to act upon him.
T3
List the five steps of the nursing process in order.
Answer
Assessment, Diagnosis, Planning, Implementation, Evaluation.
T4
List Maslow’s hierarchy of needs from lowest to highest.
Answer
Physiological → Safety & Security → Love & Belonging → Esteem → Self-Actualization.
T5
List the six links in the chain of infection.
Answer
Infectious agent, Reservoir, Portal of exit, Mode of transmission, Portal of entry, Susceptible host.
T6
State the WHO Five Moments for Hand Hygiene.
Answer
Before touching a patient; before a clean/aseptic procedure; after body-fluid exposure risk; after touching a patient; after touching the patient’s surroundings.
T7
Differentiate medical and surgical asepsis.
Answer
Medical asepsis (clean technique) reduces the number and spread of microorganisms. Surgical asepsis (sterile technique) eliminates all microorganisms including bacterial spores.
T8
Name Orem’s three nursing systems.
Answer
Wholly compensatory, Partly compensatory, Supportive-educative.
T9
List Peplau’s four phases of the nurse–patient relationship.
Answer
Orientation, Identification, Exploitation/Working, Resolution.
T10
State the approximate normal adult vital signs and the COPD SpO₂ target.
Answer
Temp ≈36.2–37.2°C; Pulse 60–100; Resp 12–20; BP ≈120/80; SpO₂ 95–100% (healthy). COPD target SpO₂ 88–92%.
T11
List the three common IM injection sites and the structure to avoid in the gluteal site.
Answer
Upper outer quadrant of buttock, outer aspect of thigh, deltoid. Avoid the sciatic nerve.
T12
State the major oxygen safety precautions.
Answer
No open flames/smoking/petroleum products; humidify ≥4 L/min; monitor for oxygen toxicity; COPD patients target SpO₂ 88–92%.
